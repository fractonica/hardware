## Bring-up, in order

1. **Provision, nothing wired.** `fr devices provision --port e8f60a81bc48 --profile s3k-fixture --alias fix-s3`: 7.7 s, addr 5, HELLO over serial, `wifi: off`. With no line wired, the CSI firmware never drove one (design §5.3 step 2).
2. **LED pin.** The fixture blinks its RGB LED 3 times at each fixture WELCOME (design D9). The user saw it on the second try: the LED is on GPIO48, the DevKitC-1 v1.0 layout. The board manifest stays as written (`leds.rgb` 48, `rgb_alt: [38]`).
3. **Pin `fw_id`.** `fixture.fw_id` in `lab.bench.yaml` is the build above.
4. **First `fr bench`.** The fixture was online over serial. Clock uncertainty F ↔ H 263–371 µs (RTT_min/2), one burst.
5. **Wiring and power-up.** The user wired as in design §6, then powered the fixture first.
   - Some lines counted edges while their DUT was still unpowered (floating): one BOOT line 1,019, two test_pin lines 717 and 122.
   - The counts stayed flat afterwards: no ongoing noise at idle.
6. **Deploy.** The M2-C1 DUT builds went out over Wi-Fi (design §5.3 step 5): all 4 updated, 11.7–23.6 s.
7. **Cap release on target** (design §4 S1, §5.3 step 6). `fr input pad-c3 boot press --layer gpio`, with no release:
   - the fixture held ch0 low for 15.000022 s (fixture edges `t_fix` 622,060,772 → 637,060,794 µs) and released it by itself at `cap_ms`;
   - the DUT that saw it reported `boot.down`, `long_press` at 600 ms, and `up` with `held_ms` 15000;
   - that DUT was pad-c3b, not pad-c3. This found the wiring swap (step 8).
8. **The wiring swap.**
   - **Probe.** For each DUT: a gpio click on its boot line, `status.set` on then off, and diag `pin_pulse 1000`, each answer looked for on every DUT.
   - **Result.** All three lines of pad-c3 and of pad-c3b were swapped: the two identical C3 boards each got the other's jumper set. pad-c6 and pad-s3 were exactly as designed.
   - **Fix.** The bench file was edited to match the wiring, with no rewire. The re-probe mapped all 12 lines correctly.

## T-HIL

### Run 1 — failed (1 of 4 DUTs passed)

No wiring phase yet.

| Phase | Result |
|---|---|
| fit | passed on all four: residual p99 1–5 µs, 0 rejected; skew +24.0 (pad-c3), +17.9 (pad-c3b), −52.0 (pad-c6), −3.6 ppm (pad-s3); DUT ISR latency 0–30 µs (pad-c6 30 µs) |
| buttons | pad-c3, pad-c3b and pad-s3: 50/50 trials, 170/170 events. **pad-c6: `double_click` failed 10 of 10** |
| leds | pad-c3b and pad-c6 passed. **pad-c3 failed:** 4-pulse blinks (one with a 0.22 ms pulse), a 100 ms on-phase split into 27.69 + 72.38 ms, 5 wrong values, 3 unexplained changes; 3 STATEs lost to RF (6.25 %, a warning on `rf_weak`). **pad-s3 failed** one blink: an on-phase of 102.03 ms against the proposed 100 ± 2 ms |
| test_pin | passed on all four: width p50 1,037 / 1,038 / 1,248 / 1,117 µs |
| cleanup | ok |

- **pad-c6's `double_click`.** The fixture saw each release on pad-c6's BOOT (external 10 kΩ pull-up) as 2–3 rising edges 11–19 µs apart. The harness paired press and release edges by ordinal, so the second `down` was paired with a release edge, about 99.85 ms "late". The DUT was right: `held_ms` 80, 80.
- **The cause of the edge trouble.** On level lines (input taps and open-drain readbacks) each transition arrived as a burst of edges about 7–15 µs apart whose recorded levels repeat, now and then with a short opposite pulse. The WS2812 lines were not affected: RMT RX has a 100 ns glitch filter.
  - Reading: the any-edge ISR re-fires while the input chatters near its threshold. Likely ground bounce over the long GND jumpers, and the S3 has no input hysteresis (inferred).
- **Changes after run 1:**
  - **A glitch filter in the hub** (`packages/hub/src/fixture/timeline.ts`, `LevelFilter`, `GLITCH_US` = 50), before an edge reaches `hil.edge`, the fit or a release's readback. It drops repeated levels and pulses shorter than 50 µs, and stamps the accepted transition at the first edge of its burst. It reconciles with the line's `level` STATE (a fresh pin read after each flush); a transition put in that way is marked `corrected: true` and is never a fit sample. `bench_get` counts per line `glitches {repeats, pulses, corrections}`. Design D9.
  - **The harness pairs press and release by level transition** on the de-glitched stream, not by ordinal (`tests/e2e/lib/hil.ts`). Design §5.2 phase 2.
  - **The blink gate widened to 100 ± 5 ms** p99, the spec's 5 ms LED rule (`LED.blinkTolMs` = 5 in `tests/e2e/lib/hil.ts`). Design §5.2 phase 3.

## Findings and open items

1. **Edge bursts on level lines; the software glitch filter is the fix.** Every transition on a `decode: level` line came as a burst 7–15 µs apart, likely ground bounce over the long GND jumpers (inferred). The hub's filter (`GLITCH_US` = 50) took it out: runs 2–4 showed no edge fault. Real phases are at least 400 µs long (bounce's shortest segment), so 50 µs leaves a wide margin. No hardware change was tried; shorter GND returns might reduce the bursts (inferred, untested). `bench_get`'s `glitches` counts show it per line.
2. **RSSI swings on pad-c3b and pad-s3** (RSSI table).
   - pad-c3b read −67…−82 dBm and pad-s3 −72…−86 dBm, from run to run.
   - pad-c3, the `rf_weak` board, read −61…−64 dBm, against −80 at M1 step 7.
   - Neither swing cost a delivery in runs 3 and 4 (0 expired, 0 timed out, 0 % loss). The `rf_weak` flag stays on pad-c3, as decided (design §7 item 7).
3. **A ~1 s Wi-Fi gap expired four commands at once** (run 2). It was handled by the one re-send; its cause on the Mac or AP side is not found.
4. **The 16-instance cap.** The fixture runs 13 instances (12 lines and its status LED; `s3k-fixture.slots.lock`: status on port 1, ch0–ch11 on ports 2–13), and 15 after M2-C3. fr_core runs at most 16 (`kMaxInstances`). A fifth wired DUT after C3 needs a raised cap and GPIO47 or a freed `rgb_alt` pin for BOOT (design §2.2).
5. **A hub stall of more than about 1 s cuts a held press on the fixture.** The fixture lets go 2 s after the hub's last frame, and the hub's keepalive comes about every 1 s. That is safe, but the trial then fails on its hold time, and the hub does not report the press as interrupted. Not seen in runs 3 and 4.
6. **The fixture shares the desk hub's power.** A hub power cut or replug, a Mac reboot, or a Mac sleep that cuts the hub's ports is a fixture power loss. With direct wiring, an unpowered fixture clamps every DUT's BOOT low, and a DUT reset then latches download mode. Run `fr bench` before touching a DUT (design D11 item 5, §6 rule 2). This bench round has no record of a fixture power loss, so the rule was not exercised.
7. **Wiring is checked only by the `wiring` phase.** Two exchanged lines both read idle, so `bench_get` cannot see a swap. Run T-HIL `--phase wiring` after any rewiring (bring-up step 8; the `hil-rig` skill says so).
8. **Risks the fix-round verifiers listed that still hold:**
   - The watchdog can reboot the fixture mid-run: with `CONFIG_ESP_TASK_WDT_PANIC=y`, a core-task callback that blocks for more than 5 s restarts it. Its lines are let go first. Not seen.
   - The pinned fixture build is the debug one, with `CONFIG_FR_TEST_HOOKS=y`. A release build exists (`024f191b5018`) and is not pinned.
   - `logs_read {selector: 'all'}` still includes the fixture's logs (`logDevices` in `packages/hub/src/ops.ts` reads every device for `all`).
   - `device_list all` still includes a bench fixture whose registry row says `node`, until its HELLO says `fixture`. Provisioning now writes the role with the row (`prepareIdentity`, I1), so this affects only older rows (inferred for fix-s3).
   - Fixture-sourced bindings are blocked by the hub only. The fixture image still runs a binding table; the hub refuses such bindings and clears the table at every handshake, but the firmware has no guard of its own.
   - `bench_get`'s newer fields (`fw_pinned`, `bad_frames`, `stale`, the edge a `release` returns, and from this round `glitches`) and `hil.edge`'s `corrected` are not named in the protocol schemas. Outputs are not hashed, so naming them later costs no REGISTRY_HASH bump (design §7, "Still open").
   - A failing bench file blocks DUT builds too: `fr gen firmware` refuses every profile the bench names (intended).
   - Editing a Kconfig file does not reconfigure existing build directories; use `--clean` when a Kconfig change matters.
9. **Load-sensitive tests, fixed at the rig.** Under a load average of about 20, `fixture-sync` and `interop-hil` failed (8 of 10 and 10 of 10 runs under CPU stress). In both, the test rigs stamped edges at wall-clock wake time. The scripted fake fixture now runs from one queue ordered by fixture time (`packages/hub/test/fixture-fake.ts`), and `fr_net_node` stamps wire inputs at the datagram's kernel arrival time. Both files then passed 10 of 10 runs under the same stress, and mutation checks showed the fit and LED assertions still catch a wrong offset, a missed rejection, a bounce taken as a sample and a 6 ms LED error. One product risk was seen but not changed: before a gesture, the driver checks the line level the hub last heard. If the hub's event loop is busy for the whole 30 ms settle window after a release, the next gesture is refused as "reads pressed at idle" (inferred; never seen on the bench).
