### (b) ESP-NOW delivery at 1 Mbps (the M0 rule)

10,000 frames per run at 100 frames/s, robust rate (11B 1M_L), node ↔ gateway both ways, node → node, node broadcasts, and one gateway → nodes broadcast run. The denominator is sent + refused.
- **Unicast: 100 % on every pair** (C3 → gw, gw → C3, C3 → C6, C6 → gw, gw → C6, C6 → S3, S3 → gw, gw → S3, S3 → C3), every frame acked, 0 refused. Echo RTT p50 4.1–5.1 ms, p99 7.7–14.8 ms, max 21.8–83.3 ms.
- **Node broadcasts: 99.96–100 %.** pad-c3's heard by gw 99.96 %, pad-c6 99.97 %; pad-c6's by gw 99.99 %, pad-s3 100 %; pad-s3's by gw 99.99 %, pad-c3 100 %.
- **Gateway broadcast: pad-c3 98.27 %, pad-c6 99.92 %, pad-s3 99.32 %.**
- **Verdict: FAIL on that one figure,** pad-c3's 98.27 % against the rule's 99 %. Why, in the lead's reading: the gateway sits at −74…−80 dBm to the AP (its first association read −80), and pad-c3 is the RF-weak board ([04 §4.6](../spec/04-boards.md)). The pair's own RSSI at pad-c3 was −65 dBm p50, so path loss alone does not explain it; the cause is not isolated further. Unicast on the same pair was 100 %: the MAC ACK and the link retries recover what a broadcast loses (*inferred*). The fix is the gateway's placement; a re-run after moving it is open.
- **200 frames/s starves a C3's TCP.** The first (b) attempts ran at 200 frames/s. pad-c3's own blast, and the gateway's blast toward it, starved its TCP session: it fell back to the gateway (its `stats gateway` opened 1 / closed 1, `too_big` 2: diag answers over one ESP-NOW frame) and the harness's diag calls timed out. The runs above are at 100 frames/s, and the harness now waits for the node to be back on TCP (it retries a diag answered over the gateway).

### (c) The 6 Mbps per-peer rate, and `game`

(b)'s runs with `air rate fast` (11G 6M) on every unicast peer and the broadcast peer, at 200 frames/s. Every board took the rate: `rate_fail` 0 on all four.
- **Unicast ≥ 99.93 %, every frame acked:** 100 % on 7 pairs; pad-c6 → pad-s3 99.93 % (7 refused by the driver, 100 % of the sent received) and pad-s3 → gw 99.98 % (2 refused). Echo RTT p50 **1.8–3.2 ms** (against 4.1–5.1 ms at 1 Mbps), p99 6.7–11.3 ms.
- **Node broadcasts 97.98–99.96 %:** pad-c3's heard by gw 97.98 % (3 refused), by pad-c6 99.75 %; pad-c6's 99.71 / 99.96 %; pad-s3's 99.67 / 99.71 %.
- **Gateway broadcast 94.15 / 96.84 / 94.49 %** (pad-c3 / pad-c6 / pad-s3): broadcast at 6 Mbps falls below the 99 % rule everywhere.

## D23: the hub loop delay explained

`LoopMonitor.stats().p99_ms` was the maximum of per-second p99s. With a 20 ms probe each second holds about 50 probes, so a second's p99 is about its worst probe, and the "60 s p99" was close to the worst single probe in the window. The quiet-Mac "p99 15–36 ms" of [08 §8.11](../spec/08-hub.md) was that figure. As built, the monitor keeps 120 s of probes and reports their true p99 (`p99_ms`, `p99_source: probes`), with the old figure as `p99_max_s_ms` and the max over the same probes. In (g), under load average about 12, the true p99 was 5.19 ms and the worst probe 80 ms; the old reading would have said 80 ms.

## Bench bugs found and fixed

1. **A false `ROLLED_BACK`.** The first deploy of the C4 node firmware reported 3 of 4 pads `ROLLED_BACK (unknown)`, although they ran the new image and had passed its health gate. The old firmware opened a gateway session the moment TCP dropped for the reboot, and its pre-reboot HELLO over the gateway started the OTA orchestrator's "back on the old image" grace timer. **Fix:** the timer is cancelled when the target image HELLOs, with a test. Re-checked: OTA of pad-c3 and pad-c6, 2/2 `SUCCEEDED`.
2. **A stale serial handle on a fast re-plug.** The gateway, re-plugged after about 1 s, re-enumerated inside the hub's 1.5 s gone-debounce, so the hub kept its old handle open and read nothing from the port for 6 min, until a USB reset. **Fix:** the serial manager reopens a port that re-enumerated inside the debounce, with a test that fails without it.
3. **`diag_exec` refused over a gateway.** The hub kept every BULK-flagged frame off a gateway session; `diag_exec` and `bind_list` are flagged BULK only because their answers may be long. **Fix:** such a command whose frame fits goes as realtime (`gateway_bulk_unflagged`); an answer that does not fit comes back `UNSUPPORTED` (`too_big`) and the hub retries once over another link. With a test.
4. **T-INPUT's default included the gateway.** Its default selector was every online device; a gateway has no BOOT button. **Fix:** the default is `online,!role:gateway`. T-INPUT sim re-run with it: passed, 170/170 on all four pads, 0 late.

The S2 harness also retries a diag answered over the gateway (`unsupported` or a timeout) once the node is back on TCP.
