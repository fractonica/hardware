# Checkpoint C4 — OTA on the C3 and C6 (2026-10-21/22)

**Covers:** roadmap checkpoint C4 ([11](../spec/11-roadmap.md)) and gates **T-OTA** and **T-PROV** ([10 §10.5](../spec/10-testing.md)). Design and contracts: [docs/design/c4-ota.md](../design/c4-ota.md).

**Setup:** hub on the MacBook (Wi-Fi, `dimas-3.local`), node HTTP on `0.0.0.0:7422`. Boards: `pad-c3` (C3 #2 `1cdbd4175cc4`, addr 1, on a USB charger), `pad-c3b` (C3 #1 `1cdbd416ba34`, addr 3, provisioned from blank in this checkpoint, on the Mac), `pad-c6` (C6 `58e6c5d55e64`, addr 2, on the Mac).

## Also verified

- `net ip_block 12` on a USB-attached `pad-c6` (TCP and UDP blocked both ways): the device stays online, serial becomes the first link, a command sent during the block is answered over USB (`link: serial`, 2 ms), TCP reconnects afterwards, no offline event.
- Images after replacing `esp_http_client` with the own socket GET: C6 61.3 % of the slot (was 67.0 %), C3 55.1 % (was 60.9 %); no `esp_http_client`, `esp-tls`, `tcp_transport` or `http_parser` in the maps.
- `fr_app_desc` at image offset 288 on all five builds (fault byte 1 only on the fault builds).

## Findings

1. **The C3 OLED boards are RF-marginal at the 8.5 dBm cap.** Link quality follows position more than firmware: `pad-c3` heard the router at −81 dBm on one Mac port (100 % ping loss, sessions dying every ~8 s), −65 on another, −78 on a charger (flapping, four OTA attempts lost) and −70 nearer the router (stable). `pad-c3b` at −76 took 52 s for an OTA that takes 15 s at −65. The C6 sat at −62…−66 throughout. The cap itself is needed for C3 #2 to associate at all (C3 checkpoint); whether a higher cap (for example 11–13 dBm) is better on these boards is an RF question for spike S2.
2. **A degraded Wi-Fi link took a USB-attached node offline** (the node sent nothing on its healthy serial session while TCP was preferred). Fixed: heartbeats go on every session, a node and the hub both move to serial when TCP has been silent for more than 2 intervals, and a request that arrives over serial is answered over serial.
3. **`esp_http_client` pulled in ~110 KB** of TLS code even with HTTPS off. Replaced by an own HTTP/1.1 GET over BSD sockets, shared with the host interop tests.
4. The adversarial review confirmed 11 more issues, all fixed before the final runs: ABORT races on the node (a re-OFFER while aborting, ABORT during VERIFY/install), hub stall detection ignoring the node's own progress, a rollback lost between attempts, `force` retries reporting success, a serial flash not cancelled, fault builds allowed over USB, and `deploy` details (`expect_log` needs a USB-attached device in M1, build failures hiding rollbacks, `needs_serial_recovery`).
