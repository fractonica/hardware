# A1 bench round: audio in and out on `mic-s3` (2026-11-02)

[Design note](../design/audio-a1.md). Board `mic-s3` (`1cdbd449ebf4`, addr 8): ESP32-S3-WROOM-1-N16R8 on a DevKitC-1-style clone with a camera connector (`esp32s3-wroom1-cam-n16r8`), profile `s3a-audio`, an INMP441-style I2S microphone (SCK 41, WS 42, SD 2, L/R to GND) and a MAX98357A amplifier (BCLK 39, LRC 40, DIN 38, Vin 5 V) with a small speaker about 5 cm from the mic. Lab hub, router mode, 16 kHz s16 mono.

## Bring-up

- **Provisioning:** over the gateway's former Mac port (gw-c6 unplugged; the fixture was not connected that day), `--profile s3a-audio --alias mic-s3`: 19.6 s, Wi-Fi joined, TCP. The first build failed twice on target-only errors the host shim hid: an unqualified `std::min` of `size_t` and `uint32_t` (`unsigned int` vs `unsigned long` on Xtensa), and `i2s_port_t`, which IDF v6.1 removed (`i2s_chan_config_t.id` is an `int`).
- **Selftest:** `mic` "the source starts and its DMA delivers", `spk` "TX channel ready".

## Findings

1. **Mic start-up transient** (first build): the first 100 ms after a start saturated (143 samples at +32767), then about 0.6 s of offset drift (100 ms means −2351, −1695, −1080, −634, −368, −114, then about 0) that the correct 20 Hz DC blocker cannot remove: it is the part's own start-up. Every `level_get` restarted the mic and read about −12 dBFS with about 130 clipped samples in a quiet room. **Fix:** a 100 ms warm-up discarded after every start, the DC blocker primed from the first kept samples, a 200 Hz settling corner gliding to `hpf_hz` over 500 ms. After it: the first 0.3 s window's mean −278, no clipping; `level_get` −35.3 dBFS, clipping 0.

2. **The INMP441 picks up the board's own Wi-Fi transmissions.** Recordings carried negative "thumps" of 2–4 ms (later tens of ms) on the ~100 ms rhythm of the audio chunk sends, with jitter. Ruled out one by one:
   - a supply dip: a capacitor across the mic's VDD/GND changed nothing;
   - the amplifier: thumps unchanged with its Vin unplugged;
   - lost DMA buffers: the shape is not a zeroed buffer through the DC blocker, and the later `stats audio` shows 0 RX overflows.

   **The TX power decides it** (diag `air txpower`, ~10 cm wires, 2 s windows after start-up):

   | TX power | RMS | deepest dip | dips |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | 8.5 dBm | −50.6 dBFS | −318 | 0 |
   | 11 dBm | −41.6 dBFS | −1,391 | 5 |
   | 13 dBm | −46.5 dBFS | −962 | 16 |
   | 15 dBm | −42.4 dBFS | −1,630 | 22 |
   | 17 dBm | −34.2 dBFS | −4,700 | 22 |
   | 20 dBm | −30.2 dBFS | −6,352 | 24 |

   The 2.4 GHz energy reaches the mic's ASIC through its wires and is rectified into the burst's envelope: an audible click per transmission (the "GSM buzz" effect). **With the mic wires cut to under 3 cm** (quiet room, 7 s): at 20 dBm the deepest thump is about −720 (about 19 dB less), 27 of them, still on the send rhythm; at 8.5 dBm none. Room noise then dominates (−44.6 vs −43.1 dBFS). **Conclusion:** flying wires are the antenna; a compact board with millimetre traces, a ground plane and RF bypass capacitors at the mic should do better still. TX power stays at the profile default (20 dBm) for now; an automatic TX power (lower while the AP is strong) is a candidate framework feature.

3. **Review** (one pass, two reviewers): hub 2 major (a disk error mid-record crashed the hub; a stalled play GET hung `audio_play`) and 5 minor; firmware 3 major (playback starved while the mic ran; play never started without PSRAM at 44.1/48 kHz; the amp's stop blocked `fr_audio` ~91 ms, overflowing the mic's 90 ms DMA), 1 blocker (the host shim behind IDF v6.1) and 5 minor. All fixed, each with a test, plus `stats audio` (`rx_overflows`, `dropped_samples`, `clipping`, `underruns`, `ring_dry`).
