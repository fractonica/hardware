## The review round

**Shape:** 7 finders and 7 skeptics over the whole change.
- 27 findings confirmed, 3 refuted.
- Skeptic severities: 1 blocker, 3 major, 23 minor. The finders had called fleet-1 and agents-docs-1 major as well.
- sim-core-1 and fleet-3 are one defect: a powered-off radio still decoded and MAC-ACKed unicasts.
- All 27 were fixed. A verifier checked each fix, with mutations on the C++ and TS fixes.

**The blocker: fw-net-1, the LOG ring on the wrong clock.**
- **The defect:**
  - fr_serial stamped each line of the net ring with `esp_log_timestamp()`;
  - Net's drain aged it against `core_.now()`, which is `esp_timer_get_time()`;
  - the age was an unsigned u32 difference.
- **The two clocks differ on target.** With LOG v1, the RTOS timestamp source and a 100 Hz tick (every pad's sdkconfig), esp_log's clock is a cycle-count base taken after the scheduler starts, plus 10 ms ticks. It runs ahead of esp_timer by the boot's start-up time.
- **Effect:** any stamp ahead of the drain's clock wrapped to about 4.29e9 ms and was dropped as `age_drop`. On a real Wi-Fi-only board every live line would have been replaced by a `fr_net: <n> log lines dropped` marker. Only the boot backlog survived, because it waits for the session to settle. `deploy {expect_log}`, T-OTA's log mode and T-AGENT (b) could not have passed on target.
- **How it was found:** from the lab hub's own logs, not on a board.
  - The finder compared diag `info`'s `uptime_ms` (esp_timer) with the serial `I (<ms>)` stamps next to it.
  - It also read the `alive … uptime_s=` lines, where both values come from one `ESP_LOGI` call. On pad-c3b, `I (39321062) … uptime_s=39320` shows esp_log ahead by at least 63 ms on the device itself.
  - The skeptic re-measured on a copy of the hub's database: pad-c3b +229…250 ms, pad-c3 +234, pad-s3 +261…270, pad-c6 +293…303 and +556…570 after an OTA boot, fix-s3 +159. So the offset was 0.15–0.57 s on every lab board.
  - It then reproduced the drop on the host with a skew matrix: a line +1, +10 or +250 ms ahead was dropped if drained at once.
- **Why no test saw it:** the host tests, the fleet's `KitLogs` and `interop-log.test.ts` all stamp and age on one clock.
- **The fix:**
  - the ring is stamped with esp_timer (`serial.cpp:223`);
  - the age is a signed difference (`log.cpp:57`), so a stamp ahead is a fresh line;
  - the marker's `(<ms>)` uses `LogSource::text_ms`, the clock the other lines' text prints;
  - new tests use stamps +1, +250 and +570 ms ahead, an age across the u32 wrap, and a source check that the target stamps the ring with esp_timer.
  - The hub's `t_log_us` (D21) now unwraps esp_log's u32 ms and takes off each boot's lead, learnt from its live lines (hub-log-1, agents-docs-6).
- **Proven on target** in the bench round below: live `alive fw=` lines arrive every 10 s from the three Wi-Fi-only boards.

**The three majors:**
- **runner-1:** a `hub_status` snapshot that failed read as zero hub lag and zero storm counters, so the storm and D15's lag rule passed with nothing measured. Such a window is now unmeasured, never a pass.
- **runner-3:** T2-FLOOD-30 and T2-FLOOD-B-30's keepers silently dropped a node that was offline at a run's start. Every run now names all its nodes (`--devices`), and the runner waits for every node to be online before each attempt.

**The minors, by area:**

## On the bench (real boards, 2026-10-26)

Same bench as M2-C1: fixture `fix-s3` on the desk hub, wired as in the [M2-C1 note](2026-10-24-m2-c1-hil.md); pad-c3, pad-c3b and pad-c6 on chargers (Wi-Fi only); pad-s3 on USB and Wi-Fi.

### 1. A real fixture power loss, and a person-free recovery

**What happened:**
1. After a pause the fixture was gone from USB; the bench had been disturbed. pad-c6 and pad-s3 were offline.
2. You re-plugged the fixture first, then powered the boards, as the power-order rule says (design m2-c1 D11 item 5).
3. `fr bench` then named pad-s3: "its USB port is present but it sends no HELLO: likely download mode".

**Reading.** This is the direct-wiring hazard of design m2-c1 D11 item 5 and §6 rule 2, happening as predicted. An unpowered fixture clamps every DUT's BOOT line low, and a DUT reset then latches download mode. No record says when pad-s3 reset (inferred: while the fixture was unpowered).

**Recovery, with no person:**
- `fr serial lease 94a99015cecc -- esptool --before no-reset --after watchdog-reset read-mac`. That is the recovery 04 §4.6 and 05 §5.16 name for a C3 or S3 in download mode. esptool reads the MAC from the ROM loader, then resets the chip with a watchdog reset. The fixture was powered by then, so BOOT read high at that reset (inferred).
- pad-s3 came back online, and `fr bench` was ok.
- This works only for a DUT whose USB the hub holds. m2-c1 D11 item 5 ("Recovery") names both ways: power-cycle the DUT, and for a C3 or S3 on USB also `esptool --after watchdog-reset`; a DUT on a charger needs a person. m2-c1 §6 rule 2 names only the power-cycle.

**What it shows:**
- **A validation of m2-c1 D11.** It is the first exercise of the power-order rule: the M2-C1 note (finding 6) had no record of a fixture power loss. The rule held: you powered the fixture first, and `fr bench` was run before any DUT was touched.
- `fr bench`'s download-mode check (USB present, no HELLO) named the board at once.
- **An argument for the MOSFET upgrade** (design m2-c1 D12, "Optional MOSFET upgrade"). With an N-MOSFET on each BOOT line, an unpowered fixture can no longer clamp BOOT, and the power-order rule stops mattering for BOOT. The cost:
  - the fixture no longer reads BOOT itself (the DUT's `boot.pressed` stands in for it, m2-c1 D12);
  - the BOOT lines become `drive: nfet_gate` / `via: nfet`, with inverted logic, so the fixture profile changes, and the fixture is reflashed and its `fw_id` re-pinned (m2-c1 §4, stream S3, `m2-c1-hil.md:556`);
  - one logic-level MOSFET per DUT (a BSS138 or 2N7002), a 1 kΩ gate resistor and a gate pull-down (4.7 kΩ on ch3, 10 kΩ elsewhere), as D12 lists.
  - pad-s3's EN line needs the MOSFET in any case.
- Whether to fit it is your decision; nothing is changed.

**The cause.** The bound reused the power cut's, 3.5 heartbeats + 200 ms. It left out `net wifi_down`'s documented 300 ms apply delay:
- the delay is in 09 §9.7, fr_net's WifiSta `kApplyDelayUs`, and the kit's `kWifiApplyDelayUs` since review fix fleet-5;
- a heartbeat sent inside the 300 ms window moves the hub's 3.5-interval timeout (25 ms tick) with it.

**The correction** (*proposed*, design §7 item 5):
- `tests/sim/t2-churn.ts` `WIFI_APPLY_DELAY_MS` and `wifiDownOfflineMaxMs`: 3,500 + 300 + 200 = 4,000 ms at a 1 s heartbeat;
- a regression test in `e2e-gates-f.test.ts` pins 4,000 ms and the 3,747.7 ms case.

**The re-run** passed under the corrected bound. Its slowest `wifi_down`, 3,673.3 ms, is also inside the original 3,700 ms. So the gate's pass does not depend on the correction. Only the full tier's verdict does: under the corrected bound its event 27 (3,747.7 ms) would have passed.

## Findings and open items

1. **A clock only a board has cannot be tested on one host clock.** fw-net-1 passed every host, fleet and interop test, because they all stamp and age on one clock. It was caught by reading the lab hub's logs. Any rule that compares a board-only clock (esp_log's) with esp_timer needs an on-target check. LOG over TCP now has one (bench §3).
2. **The fixture power-loss rule is real.** It happened once, as m2-c1 D11 and §6 rule 2 predicted, and `fr bench` named it. The recovery needs a person unless the DUT is on USB; on USB, the `esptool --after watchdog-reset` hint of 04 §4.6 and 05 §5.16 worked through a serial lease. m2-c1 §6 rule 2 names only the power-cycle, while D11 item 5 also names esptool for a C3 or S3 on USB. The MOSFET upgrade (m2-c1 D12) removes the hazard for BOOT; it is your decision.
3. **Proposed rules awaiting your approval:** D19's ≥ 99 %; T2-NN-30's staggered running phase (lockstep published only); the T2-CHURN-30 `wifi_down` bound +300 ms; the inconclusive-rule numbers; and the other thresholds the design marks *proposed* (design §7 items 4 and 5).
4. **The proposed M3 deferrals in 11 §11.6** are still your decision, T2 in PR CI among them.
5. **15 pairs in lockstep at 30 nodes** put 15 unicast exchanges into one contention burst at 1 Mbps. The full tier measured RTT p50 17–53 ms against the 10 ms rule. That is model output, not RF evidence: C4's calibration has not happened.
6. **The wall-clock seed count runs above the virtual one** (6.70 against 5.35–5.49 at one cut node). Both are under 8; the cause is not investigated.
7. **RSSI:** pad-c3b and pad-s3 read −70…−76 dBm across the bench round, with no delivery cost (bench §6).
8. **Load-sensitive tests:** the two T-LAT vitest files fail at a 1-min load of about 580 and pass alone, as before.
9. **OTA cut on loopback.** The hub serves the whole image before any cut. The sim's cut therefore waits on the node's stored offset. It keeps T-OTA's assumption that, on a real board, 256 KB served means a 64 KB stride is in flash.
