## 1. Scope

**In C2**
- **C++ host kit** (`firmware/test/host/sim/`, library `fr_sim`, namespace `frsim`):
  - the N-node medium (`Medium`): per-pair reach, loss, RSSI, duplication and latency; airtime at 1 and 6 Mbps; a channel per radio; carrier sense and collisions; channel-use stats;
  - each node's radio (`SimAir`) over the real `air::Scheduler`;
  - one core per node (`SimCore`) and an `Executor` with a virtual and a wall-clock mode;
  - the node classes taken out of `net_node.cpp` (Device, NodeCap, NodeDiag, NodeOtaHost, blobs, state printers, the command table), plus `FleetNode`, the links `SimTcp`/`SimUdp`/`SimSerial`, an AP model and a serial-bridge server;
  - `fr_fleet`, one process hosting N nodes against a private hub.
- **T2-SCALE** in `pnpm test:host`: 10 and 30 nodes in virtual time, with a C++ hub model.
- **Firmware:** the flood-rate refund (`air.cpp`, `peer.cpp`); hub-epoch adoption in router mode (`air.cpp on_hub_air`); LOG frames over TCP (`fr_net`, `fr_serial`, `fr_core`).
- **Schema:** `LOG.LINE.text` 200 → 384; HELLO caps `b6 net_log`.
- **Hub:** the scale fixes the fleet finds at 30+ (handshake storm, DIRECTORY on HELLO, fan-out cost, the dashboard's 30 tiles); fan-out on every session; LOG ingest with dedup; `deploy`'s log-source rule; scale counters in `hub_status`; `test_run` tier `sim`.
- **Harness:** the private-hub module, YAML scenarios, the sim runner, a fleet diag, a person bot, the T2-CHURN-30 driver, and small backward-compatible changes to `t-flood.ts`, `t-flood-b.ts`, `t-input.ts` and `t-ota.ts`.
- **Agents:** the "logs only over USB" rule in the four plugin skills, `AGENTS.md` and 09.

**Not in C2**
- gateway topologies and gates (T2-GW-*, C6); the gateway role on this medium (C4: a `FleetNode` role in `fr_fleet`, using the channels built here, D24);
- T2-ANALOG-30 (C3, after C4's spike S2), though the medium's channel-use stats are built here for it;
- LOG over the gateway, and epoch adoption from BEACON (C6);
- CSA and channel changes on the AP model (C4/C6): C2's AP is on/off, latency, loss and gaps;
- per-node clock offsets and skew in use (C8): `SimCore` has the knobs, default 0;
- multi-hop beyond router mode's one air layer (TTL 2): published only, never gated (D10);
- a virtual fixture inside the fleet: it stays in `fr_net_node`, one per process (D1);
- T2 in PR CI: the M2-C2 row says "laptop runs only, until a CI target exists". Moving it to M3 is one of §11.6's *proposed* deferrals, which still waits for your decision;
- the `game` rate decision (C4): the medium models 6 Mbps so C4 and C6 can use it;
- calibrating the medium against real RF: C4's S2 (b)/(c) measure it; until then T2's transmission counts are model output.

## 2. Decisions

| # | Decision |
|---|---|
| D1 | **One process, one thread: `fr_fleet` hosts N nodes, the medium, the AP and the executor.**<br>**Why one process.** N `fr_net_node` processes cost 2–3 fds each, N schedulers, and a MAC ACK carried as a UDP datagram inside a 3 ms window (`net_node.cpp:838-840`). That window is why `interop-air` accepts "at least three quarters espnow" on a loaded host. In one process the medium decides MAC ACKs itself.<br>**Why one thread.** It keeps Net's core-task contract (`net.hpp:350`) with no locks, and makes virtual time deterministic. CPU (*inferred*): at 10 hub broadcasts/s, about 6 transmissions each, heard by 29 nodes, is about 2,000 `receive_air`/s, plus about 300 TCP frames/s and 60 heartbeats/s: a few % of a core even under ASan.<br>**N Nets in one process are safe.** Net keeps its state in members (`net.hpp:869-972`). The anonymous namespaces of `net.cpp:33-57`, `air.cpp:24-53`, `peer.cpp:23-32` and `bindings.cpp:24-43` hold only constants and pure functions. The survey's grep found no logging, `getenv` or I/O in `fr_net/src/core`, `fr_ota/src/core`, `fr_core/src/services`, `fr_caps/src` or `fr_proto/src`. Sizes (*survey*, a probe against the headers): Net 25,592 B, Scheduler 4,648 B, so 60 nodes take about 1.8 MB. A new host test runs two Nets with interleaved traffic and checks each against a solo run, so a future function-local static fails loudly.<br>**What collides, and how the fleet avoids it:**<br>• `fr_core`'s target runtime (`core.cpp:84-162`, `:325-347`, `:600-741`), `fr::core::start/run/sink` and `fr::msg::send/on` (`msg.hpp:38`, `:52`): never built on the host (`CMakeLists.txt:50-59`). Fleet nodes use `fr::core::msg_send(net, …)` and a per-node `MsgHandlers` (`node_net.hpp:123-178`);<br>• the free functions `fr::core::link_info()` and `hub_session()` (`node_net.hpp:97`, `:100`): only `FixtureService` calls `link_info` (`fixture_service.cpp:28`, `:169`), and the fleet never hosts one. Net uses the per-node `Host::link_info` (`net.hpp:195`);<br>• the fixture drivers' statics (`fixture_gpio.cpp:43-54`, `ws2812_rx.cpp:25-27`, `esp_sim.cpp:107-109`): only `fr_host_tests` links them; the virtual fixture stays in `fr_net_node`;<br>• `net_node.cpp`'s process-wide `g_node`, untagged stdout, one stdin and one clock (`:167-174`): replaced in the fleet by a per-node emitter, a tagged command channel (D8) and per-node cores (D6). `ota_fake::emit` (`ota_fake.hpp:36`), which MemFlash and HttpFetch call deep inside, routes through a current-node context that the executor sets at every entry point (timers, posts, fd callbacks, medium deliveries), `thread_local` in worker threads.<br>**Threads only for blocking OTA downloads.** `HttpGet` blocks up to 5 s per read (`ota_fake.hpp:619-640`). Jobs cannot be routed by kind, because fr_worker jobs are opaque `Callback<void(), 32>` (`ota_fake.hpp:232-257`). fr_ota also posts `step()` (fetch plus flash), `op_mark_valid`, `op_rollback`, `op_commit` and `store->clear` to one worker and relies on FIFO, one-at-a-time execution, as on the single fr_worker task (`ota.cpp:209`, `:449-463`, `:533`). Splitting them would race MemFlash and MemStore. **So in wall mode each node's worker is one serial strand on a pool of 16 threads** (ota_deploy's highest `max_parallel`): all of that node's jobs run on it in post order, one at a time. Core-bound work goes back through the thread-safe `SimCore::post` (a mutex and a wake of the executor's waiter, D6). In virtual mode the strand runs inline, as `HostWorker` does. The pool is needed before T2-OTA-30, since one stalled read would freeze all 30 nodes.<br>**fds.** Per node: one non-blocking TCP socket, plus a bridge connection for a USB node. UDP: one shared socket by default (D7). So 30 nodes need about 37 fds and 60 need about 67, plus at most 16 HTTP sockets. `fr_fleet` raises its own `RLIMIT_NOFILE` soft limit to min(hard, need) and prints both.<br>**60+ nodes:** the same loop. If the lag p99 nears 100 ms, the next step is one process per channel (they share no air), not threads (*inferred*).<br>**Rejected:** a thread per node (locks in every Net call, nondeterminism) and a threaded medium (it needs conservative synchronisation with a 130 µs lookahead, the shortest 6 Mbps frame). |
| D2 | **The shared kit, extracted from `net_node.cpp`.** Everything in `net_node.cpp` lives in one anonymous namespace (`:155-2422`).<br>**Moves into `fr_sim`:** Device, NodeCap, NodeDiag, NodeOtaHost, FileBlob and a MemBlob, the state printers (`print_state`, `c5_state`, `c6_state`, `print_neighbors`, `print_directory`, `stdin_msg`, `serial_diag_id`), and the stdin command table (`:2754-2845`) as `Commands::run(NodeCtx&, std::string_view)`.<br>**The seam is `NodeCtx`:** the node's Net, stores and services, plus a `NodeLinks` interface for what the hooks touch (`wifi_down`, `tcp 0/1`, `udp_block`, `ip_block`, air loss, the air stats). `fr_net_node` implements `NodeLinks` over its own TcpLink, UdpLink, SerialLink and FakeAir; `FleetNode` over SimTcp, SimUdp, SimSerial, SimAir and the AP. **The two differ on purpose in one place:** `fr_net_node`'s `wifi_down` calls `tcp->enable(false)`, which `::close`s the socket (`net_node.cpp:2623-2642`, `:620-626`, `:690-700`). The hub then sees a FIN and offlines a TCP-only node at once with `link_lost` (`router/index.ts:1189-1199`). The fleet's `wifi_down`, `tcp 0`, AP down and `power off` never close the hub socket (D7), because on RF no FIN arrives. In both, `net wifi_down` over fr.diag switches Wi-Fi 300 ms after its RESULT, as fr_net's WifiSta does (`kApplyDelayUs`, `wifi_sta.hpp:47`), so that RESULT leaves over TCP first; the stdin `wifi_down` stays immediate (review fleet-5).<br>**Stays in `net_node.cpp`:** `main()`, argv parsing, HostCore and the `poll()` loop, its blocking TcpLink, UdpLink and SerialLink, FakeAir and its registry files, the wire and tap classes (WiredButton, PadLight, UdpOut, `recv_stamped`), the fixture mode and its `link_info()` definition. So `fr_net_node`'s I/O timing does not change, and the 3-node interop suites keep FakeAir as §11.6 says.<br>**Output.** `Out` is a per-node emitter. In `fr_net_node` it prints exactly today's untagged lines, which `cpp-node.ts` and every interop suite parse. In the fleet it tags each line with `"node":"<alias>"`.<br>**Stable references.** Device copies Options while FakeAir, NodeDiag and NodeOtaHost hold `const Options &` (`:1919` against `:1155`, `:1480`, `:1987`). Each FleetNode owns one `NodeSpec` behind a `unique_ptr`, so references survive the fleet's vectors growing.<br>**Order.** The extraction lands first, as a pure refactor with the interop and `e2e-gates-*` suites green, before any fleet code.<br>**Rules:** namespace `frsim` (no clash with `frt::` in `tests/`); no exceptions (`-fno-exceptions`, `CMakeLists.txt:42`), so no `std::stoi`; cJSON (vendored, `fr_cjson`) for the fleet's spec file. |
| D3 | **The medium: a directed pair table, a counter-based RNG.** `frsim::Medium` replaces nothing in C2 (D23); it is new code.<br>**Pairs:** `Pair{reach, loss, rssi_dbm, rssi_sigma_db, dup, lat_min_us, lat_max_us}` for each (tx, rx). Filled from:<br>• `one_room`: every pair in reach; RSSI drawn per pair from a range (default −85…−45 dBm), with up to 3 dB asymmetry; loss drawn per pair from a range (default 0.1–1.5 %, C5's bench range) and **independent of RSSI**;<br>• `two_rooms`: two clusters, full reach inside, partial reach across;<br>• `matrix`: an explicit table;<br>• `positions`: log-distance path loss, RSSI = P0 − 10·n·log10(d) with n ≈ 2.5–3, and loss from RSSI through a logistic around the sensitivity (about −95 dBm at 1 Mbps, −90 dBm at 6 Mbps). These constants are *inferred* and uncalibrated, so `positions` is for published runs only until C4's S2 (b) measures delivery against RSSI.<br>**Why RSSI varies even when loss does not:** with one RSSI for every pair (`net_air_medium.hpp:28`), `rssi_bias` (`air.cpp:31-35`) is the same constant on every node, so relay ordering by distance is never exercised.<br>**RNG:** `u = splitmix64(seed ^ H(sender_mac, sender_frame_no, receiver_mac, purpose))`, purpose ∈ {loss, ack, dup, latency, rssi, backoff}. A frame's fate at a receiver therefore does not move when N or an unrelated event changes, unlike today's one `mt19937` (`net_air_medium.hpp:215`), which also draws the backoff and the hub latency. Timing still depends on the other traffic. Net's own jitter RNG is seeded per scenario seed too (`rng_seed = mix(seed, MAC)`, D9), so a seed varies the relay jitter as well as the medium. |
| D4 | **Airtime, carrier sense, collisions, channels, channel use.** The formulas are in §2.1 and reproduce every row of 05 §5.15.<br>**Rate:** per sender and destination, as `espnow.cpp:108-118` sets it per peer: the broadcast peer and the unicast peers separately. Router mode runs 1 Mbps (`WIFI_PHY_RATE_1M_L`); 6 Mbps is there for C4 and C6.<br>**Channels:** each radio has one. Radios on different channels neither hear, sense nor collide.<br>**Carrier sense** (a simplified DCF): a sender defers while any on-air frame it can hear is running, waits DIFS plus a backoff of U{0..CW} slots, and freezes the backoff while the channel is busy. Two senders whose backoff ends in the same slot both transmit.<br>**Collisions** (default on for T2): at a receiver, overlapping frames from senders in reach collide; a frame stronger than every overlapping one by ≥ `capture_db` (10, *inferred*) survives. Hidden terminals follow from partial reach. `collisions: off` reproduces the C5 medium (D23). The gated transmission bounds use `collisions: off` until a collisions-on bound is approved (D10, Q2).<br>**Reception:** at on-air end + U(lat_min, lat_max) (default 50–200 µs, *inferred* driver and task latency), in order per (tx, rx), into the receiver's 16-slot ring (05 §5.3; overflow counted as `rx_lost`). As on target, the ring is drained by one coalesced service request on the receiver's network queue, 4 frames per service, re-requested while frames remain (`espnow.cpp:15`, `:35-37`, `:80-99`, `:198-211`). ### 2.1 The airtime model (D4)

Each frame's on-air time is separate from the contention before it. 43 B of ESP-NOW overhead: MAC header 24, category 1, OUI 3, random 4, vendor element 7, FCS 4 (*inferred* for a 1470 B v2 frame with 6 vendor elements, where it is approximate).

| | 1 Mbps (11b, long preamble) | 6 Mbps (11g OFDM, short slot) |
|---|---|---|
| On air, `len` B of envelope | 192 + 8·(len + 43) µs | 20 + 4·⌈(22 + 8·(len + 43)) / 24⌉ + 6 µs |
| Unicast ACK (after SIFS 10) | + 314 µs (SIFS + 192 + 112) | + 60 µs |
| Contention | DIFS 50 + U{0..31} × 20 µs (mean 360) | DIFS 28 + U{0..15} × 9 µs (mean 96) |
| 32 B broadcast | 792 + 360 = 1,152 µs (05 §5.15: 1.15 ms) | 130 + 96 = 226 µs (0.23 ms) |
| 32 B unicast | 1,466 µs (1.47 ms) | 286 µs (0.29 ms) |
| 1470 B | 12,296 + 360 = 12,656 µs (~12.6 ms) | 2,050 + 96 = 2,146 µs (~2.1 ms) |

The C5 medium's 900 + 8·len µs (`net_air_medium.hpp:108`) is the 1 Mbps row with the mean contention folded in: 1,156 µs at 32 B.

## 6. What you do

Nothing. No board moves, no wiring, no person in any gate. The person bot stands in for every PLEASE line, and the bench regressions of §5.1 need no person.

**`ulimit`:** §11.6 asks for `ulimit -n` above about 60 nodes because macOS's launchd soft limit is 256. It is not needed:
- `fr_fleet` raises its own soft `RLIMIT_NOFILE` to min(hard, need) and prints both (about 37 fds at 30 nodes, 67 at 60, D1);
- Node raises its own soft limit to the hard limit at start-up (*inferred* from Node's start-up code; the private hub holds about N accepted sockets plus its own);
- this sandbox shell reported a soft limit of 1,048,576 and `kern.maxfilesperproc` 245,760 (survey).

The runner fails fast with ENV_MISSING, naming `ulimit -n`, only if the hard limit is below need (about 3N + 64). The C2 row of "What you provide" becomes "Nothing" (§8).
